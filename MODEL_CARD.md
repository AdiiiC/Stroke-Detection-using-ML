# Model Card — Stroke Risk Prediction

> **Disclaimer:** This model is a research and educational artifact. It is
> **not** a medical device, has not undergone clinical validation, and must
> **not** be used to make real patient-care decisions.

## Model details
- **Task:** Binary classification — probability of stroke from tabular
  demographic/clinical risk factors.
- **Architecture:** scikit-learn / imbalanced-learn pipeline:
  domain feature engineering → `ColumnTransformer` (median/most-frequent
  imputation, standardisation, one-hot) → optional SMOTE (train-fold only)
  → gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM by default) → probability calibration
  (isotonic).
- **Hyperparameters:** tuned with Optuna (TPE) maximising cross-validated
  PR-AUC.
- **Optional imaging branch:** EfficientNet-B0 / ResNet CNN on brain CT
  with Grad-CAM saliency; U-Net for MRI lesion segmentation; late fusion
  (weighted or stacked) of tabular + imaging probabilities.

## Intended use
- Educational demonstration of a rigorous, explainable, fair ML workflow
  for an imbalanced clinical problem.
- **Out of scope:** clinical triage, diagnosis, or any deployment that
  influences patient care.

## Data
- **Tabular:** Kaggle stroke-prediction dataset (~15.3k rows, ~4.1% positive).
  Features: age, gender, hypertension, heart disease, marital status, work
  type, residence, average glucose, BMI, smoking status.
- **Imaging (optional):** Kaggle Brain Stroke CT dataset (download via
  `scripts/download_data.py`); ISLES/AISD MRI for segmentation (manual).

## Metrics (validation split)
Headline metrics are **ROC-AUC**, **PR-AUC**, and **recall** at a
recall-prioritised operating threshold (a missed stroke is far costlier
than a false alarm). Accuracy is intentionally **not** a headline metric
because the base rate is ~4%. See `artifacts/reports/metrics.json` and the
figures in `artifacts/figures/` (ROC/PR, calibration, confusion matrix,
SHAP summary) generated by each training run.

## Fairness
Subgroup performance (recall, selection rate, AUC) is audited across
gender, age band, residence, and work type. Disparities are written to
`artifacts/reports/fairness_audit.csv` and
`artifacts/reports/fairness_disparities.csv`. Small subgroups (e.g.
pediatric, "Never_worked") have unstable estimates and are flagged rather
than acted upon automatically.

## Calibration
Probabilities are calibrated (isotonic) so a predicted "30% risk"
corresponds to ~30% observed frequency. Quality is tracked via the Brier
score and the reliability curve.

## Ethical considerations & limitations
- Only public, non-identifiable competition data is used.
- The dataset under-represents some groups; predictions for rare subgroups
  are less reliable.
- The model captures correlation, not causation; it must never be used to
  deny care or coverage.
- Distribution shift (different population/hospital) will degrade
  performance; monitor before any reuse.
